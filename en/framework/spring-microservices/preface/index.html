Preface to Spring Microservices Architecture

Starting from cloud computing, scalability, and software architecture, this preface lays out the core questions enterprises need to understand when adopting a microservices architecture.

Why This Preface

Microservices are not “smaller monoliths” — they are a synchronized restructuring of organizational, team, and system boundaries.
This preface clarifies a few core questions up front, so microservices don’t get treated as a universal framework.

Why Do Enterprises Adopt Microservices?

Common drivers include:

  • Complexity layering: once a monolith grows beyond a certain scale, the cost of change rises sharply
  • Faster delivery cadence: teams can deliver different business domains in parallel
  • Availability and elasticity: resources can scale in and out at the granularity of individual services
  • Separation of operations and governance: monitoring, tracing, authentication, and gateways can evolve incrementally by domain

Conway’s Law (Melvin R. Conway, 1968) reminds us that organizational structure influences system structure.
The way teams communicate determines service boundaries, and service boundaries in turn constrain collaboration efficiency.

What to Clarify Before Moving from Monolith to Microservices

  • Whether business boundaries are clear and can be stably abstracted into contexts
  • How data boundaries are defined and whether ownership is explicit
  • Whether observability (monitoring, logging, and distributed tracing) can be rolled out in sync
  • Whether the release process and rollback capability can support multi-service deployments

First, Distinguish Two Key Points

Object-oriented thinking is more of a “modeling mindset,” while microservices lean more toward “organizational structure” and “operating model.”
Without consistent modeling, no matter how many components you pile on, the system will still become hard to maintain.

Spring Beans and Dependency Governance

In microservices, the Spring ecosystem’s IoC/DI remains the default paradigm, but you need to make clear trade-offs between service granularity, transaction boundaries, and client-side load balancing.
Leading with “technical capabilities” isn’t necessarily right — defining the “problem boundary” first matters more.
