How to Learn?

A practical, continuously evolving approach to learning, from identifying information and internalizing knowledge to turning it into capability.

This is a broad topic. Here I share the insights I’ve gathered about learning, stage by stage.

The logic of learning

Learning means taking in a lot of information, turning it into knowledge, and then converting a small part of that knowledge into capability.

How do you identify good information?

  1. It’s well written.
  2. It’s reliable, authoritative, and influential. For example, Robert C. Martin and Martin Fowler.

In business intelligence (BI), an ETL pipeline performs extraction, cleaning, transformation, and loading to bring useful data into the data warehouse — and the same applies to identifying good information.

From knowledge to capability

Practice empathy: put yourself in the other person’s situation, or their “position” (perspective-taking). Understand and share their feelings, emotions, thoughts, and motives — feel what they feel.

If someone has a 39 °C fever, for example, don’t just remind them to rest and drink plenty of water — think about how miserable they feel right now, and how cold they must be. Step into their situation, and the questions you ask, the words you say, and the things you do are no longer the same.

Build life experience. Life experience = what you’ve been through + what you’ve learned from it

Knowledge you gain firsthand — from what you’ve personally seen, heard, and done — is exactly the lessons-learned part.

Life experience lets you turn knowledge into capability, bit by bit.

How do you deal with learning anxiety?

Adjust your mindset and avoid these four: the quantity mindset, the quick-fix mindset, the passive mindset, and the entertainment mindset.

The quantity mindset

Reading isn’t a numbers game.

As long as it helps you and you learn something from it, that’s enough.

The quick-fix mindset

Learning is like a marathon — only longer.

We often expect results right away, but many things don’t pay off immediately — they change you gradually.

The passive mindset

Avoid the passive mindset. Many people today watch short videos (Douyin, WeChat Channels, etc.) and are basically passive consumers. It’s like watching a soap opera: it comes and goes, and nothing sticks.

The entertainment mindset

Killing time is not the purpose of learning. You have to calm down and focus.

What struck me most is Ji Xianlin during his studies in Germany: for ten full years, he basically led a dorm-to-library life.

How do you build learning capability?

Put in the hard work

There’s a famous line in the classic dialogue of Farewell My Concubine: “To shine in public, you must suffer in private.”

Pay attention to the resources around you

Good films, TV series, and novels help you develop the ability to understand other people.

For example, in Heidi, some classic lines:

  1. People love to talk about evil things. You need to judge for yourself and trust your own eyes and ears.

  2. If something in life makes you happy, then just do it! Don’t mind what others say.

  3. Everyone laughs at me because I want to write stories.

  4. If you could read, you would know what happens next in the story. You love stories, don’t you?

  5. They know too little, while you have seen many things. If doing something makes you happy, then just do it, no matter what others say.

For example, in The Distant Savior:

本是后山人，偶做前堂客。

醉舞经阁半卷书，坐井说天阔。

大志戏功名，海斗量福祸。

论到囊中羞涩时，怒指乾坤错。

(A classical poem from the novel, kept in the original Chinese.)

Learn selectively

In the age of the knowledge explosion, you have to find the knowledge that’s useful to you within a sea of knowledge.

Not all knowledge is equally important. You have to learn to pick and choose.

Life today is fast-paced.

  1. If an article is too long, no one wants to read it — a few minutes should be enough to finish it.
  2. Videos that are too long get even fewer viewers — and overly long films are no exception.
  3. Spend less time on your phone, because it degrades your abilities (weakening both your imagination and your endurance).

Learn within architecture

Study good frameworks.

Learn by teaching

Teach others what you’ve learned. You’ve truly mastered something only when others can learn it from you.

Should you take notes while reading?

Some books only need a light read or a quick skim — news, current events, or entertainment, for example — with no notes required.

Other books may need underlining and notes.

And some books gnaw at you until you write about them — those deserve a full written reflection.

Output is always the best way to learn.

In the book Creative Selection:

The seven elements behind Apple’s success:

(1) Inspiration: use your imagination and boldly envision what could be possible.

(2) Collaboration: work well with others, share what you have, and complement each other’s strengths.

(3) Craft: practice repeatedly until you achieve high-quality results, always refining.

(4) Diligence: keep doing seemingly boring but necessary repetitive work — don’t rely on shortcuts, and don’t cut corners on effort.

(5) Decisiveness: make firm decisions and refuse to postpone or delay.

(6) Taste: develop sharp judgment and look for the balance that makes the whole harmonious and pleasing.

(7) Empathy: try to see the world from other people’s perspective and create great products that fit their lives and meet their needs.
